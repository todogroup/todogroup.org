Meet the candidates for the **2027–2029 Steering Committee term**. Explore each candidate’s background, community experience, and proposed priorities below.

These profiles reproduce the information in the [candidate guide (PDF)](/files/TODO-Group-2027-Steering-Committee-Candidates.pdf), published in October 2026. Candidates appear in the same order as the guide.

## The 2027 election

The next term focuses on practical OSPO skills, AI governance, and stronger connections across open ecosystems:

- **Connect open ecosystems:** Build relationships across communities and bring OSPO adoption and governance needs into shared work.
- **Bring practice into AI governance:** Share experience with AI-assisted development, agentic workflows, standards, and open source AI infrastructure.
- **Turn priorities into action:** Maintain resources, support working groups and events, and mentor practitioners with community input.

## David Walter

**Kiteworks**  
Vice President, Open Source Program Office

### Background

David Walter leads Kiteworks’ Open Source Program Office, stewarding open source projects and shaping its digital sovereignty strategy. A contributor to the ownCloud ecosystem since 2014, David brings experience on both the community and corporate sides of open source. David’s work connects engineering, legal, procurement, product, and executive teams.

### Contributions and community experience

David has participated in TODO meetings and presented at the 2026 TODO Group Summit in Prague. At Kiteworks, David has helped establish a new OSPO with a published governance charter, a Developer Certificate of Origin contribution model, and a policy for AI-assisted contributions.

### Proposed priorities

David proposes keeping TODO’s guidance grounded in the practical experience of running an OSPO, including lessons from programs that are still developing. David’s priorities include digital sovereignty and procurement needs for European and public-sector OSPOs, alongside contribution policies for AI-generated code.

### Perspective for 2027

David would bring experience translating community expectations into organizational governance, with a focus on practical policy and sustained dialogue between companies and contributors.

## Diego Mastroianni

**Cloudera**  
Open Source Program Office Lead

### Background

Diego Mastroianni leads Cloudera’s OSPO, setting open source strategy and overseeing participation across a community of more than 200 contributors in data and AI ecosystems. Diego combines a PhD in Management focused on open source governance with a Computer Engineering degree and nearly two decades of technology experience.

### Contributions and community experience

Diego co-chairs TODO’s Agentic AI to Empower OSPOs Working Group, has delivered a community show-and-tell presentation, and contributed to the 2026 State of OSPOs and Open Source Management report. Diego’s internal Open Source Hub uses a knowledge graph to connect contributors, projects, events, and resources.

### Proposed priorities

Diego proposes improving working-group onboarding so new participants can find materials, follow discussions, and identify useful contributions. Diego also wants to keep the OSPO Book current and explore an open source version of the Open Source Hub as part of a practical toolkit for OSPO managers.

### Perspective for 2027

Diego would connect governance research with hands-on OSPO operations and experience using agentic coding tools, while preserving TODO’s welcoming culture and shared learning.

## Dr. Islam Gouda

**Humanized AI Economy Initiative**  
Founder; AI Governance and Digital Transformation Researcher

### Background

Dr. Islam Gouda researches AI governance and organizational transformation and founded the Humanized AI Economy Initiative. Islam’s work examines human oversight, delegated authority, and organizational readiness for agentic AI. Islam developed the Human and Organizational Readiness for Trusted Agentic AI framework.

### Contributions and community experience

Islam reports no direct TODO contributions to date. Islam’s related experience includes engagement with the ITU-T Focus Group on Trust and Identity for Humans and Agentic AI and IEEE AI standards initiatives. Islam also participates in AI education and workforce development through the Global Alliance for Artificial Intelligence.

### Proposed priorities

Islam proposes translating governance concepts into practical case studies and implementation guidance for OSPOs. Islam would encourage exchange on AI-generated code, licensing, attribution, and review practices, and broaden participation across sectors and regions, including the Global South. Islam emphasizes listening to existing members before shaping an agenda.

### Perspective for 2027

Islam would bring an interdisciplinary perspective on AI oversight and open standards, connecting organizational accountability with practitioner-led knowledge sharing.

Natali seeks to continue Natali’s liaison work, incorporate practitioner needs into educational resources, and explore closer collaboration with TODO Ambassadors. ### Perspective for 2027

Natali’s stewardship of AI projects at Cisco, including submissions to the Linux Foundation and CNCF sandbox, would inform peer discussions on AI governance, project maintenance, and open standards.

## Voting with LFX PCC

The 2027 TODO Steering Committee election will use the **LFX Project Control Center (PCC)** voting tool. General Member voting is limited to the primary contacts of TODO Group General Members.

1. **Confirm your voting contact.** Eligible voting contacts will receive an email from TODO to confirm that they are the designated voting contact for their organization. Please review the message and confirm or provide any corrections.
2. **Look for the LFX voting email.** Once the poll is created and activated in LFX PCC, eligible voting contacts will receive an automated email from LFX with instructions for casting their vote.
3. **Submit your organization’s vote.** Follow the instructions in the LFX email and complete your ballot before the voting deadline.

### Election and appointment timeline

| Date | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| October 21–November 20, 2026 | General Member voting: primary contacts only |
| November 22, 2026 | Elected members announced |
| December 1, 2026 | Appointed members confirmed by the current Steering Committee |
| December 10, 2026 | Onboarding begins |
| January 1, 2027 | New terms begin |
